## Daily Intake Targets

The baseline recommendation for sedentary adults sits at 0.8 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. This figure represents the minimum required to avoid deficiency, not the amount associated with optimal outcomes.

The Vandermeer Study (2019) followed 340 resistance-training adults over eighteen months and found that intakes of 1.6 grams per kilogram per day maximised lean mass retention. Intakes above 2.2 grams per kilogram produced no additional benefit in any measured outcome, and the authors described the range beyond that point as nutritionally inert rather than harmful.

Older adults form an important exception. Vandermeer reported that participants over sixty-five required approximately 1.2 grams per kilogram simply to match the nitrogen balance that younger participants achieved at 0.8 grams per kilogram, a phenomenon the authors attributed to anabolic resistance.

## The Leucine Threshold

Protein synthesis does not respond linearly to protein intake. It behaves closer to a switch, and leucine is what flips it.

A meal must deliver roughly 2.5 grams of leucine to trigger a maximal muscle protein synthesis response. Below that threshold, the response is muted regardless of how much total protein the meal contains. This is why 20 to 40 grams of high-quality protein per meal is the commonly cited target: that range reliably clears the leucine threshold for most protein sources.

Whey protein is unusually leucine-dense at approximately 11 percent leucine by weight, meaning around 23 grams of whey clears the threshold. Most plant proteins sit closer to 7 percent, which is why larger portions are typically recommended for plant-based diets.

## Distribution Across the Day

Total daily protein matters most, but distribution matters more than it is usually given credit for.

The Vandermeer Study compared participants consuming 120 grams of protein in two meals against participants consuming the same 120 grams across four meals. The four-meal group showed measurably better lean mass outcomes despite identical total intake. The mechanism is straightforward: each meal that clears the leucine threshold triggers a separate synthesis event, and two large meals trigger two events where four moderate meals trigger four.

The practical guidance that follows is to spread protein across three to four meals of 25 to 40 grams each, rather than concentrating it in a single large evening meal.

## Protein Quality

Not all protein sources are equivalent. Quality is usually measured by the Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score, or DIAAS, which accounts for both amino acid profile and actual digestibility.

Animal proteins generally score above 1.0. Whole egg scores approximately 1.13 and whey isolate approximately 1.09. Most single plant sources score below 0.8, with wheat protein around 0.45 due to limited lysine.

Combining complementary plant sources across the day resolves this. Legumes are rich in lysine and limited in methionine; grains are the reverse. Eaten across a day, the combined profile is comparable to animal sources, and the older advice that they had to be eaten in the same meal has not held up.
