# Micronutrients and Absorption

*Synthetic document written for RAG testing. Named studies and figures are fictional and should not be treated as health information.*

## Population Deficiencies

The Brightwater Survey (2021) measured serum micronutrient levels across 8,900 adults and found that four deficiencies accounted for the overwhelming majority of cases.

Vitamin D was the most common by a wide margin: 41 percent of participants fell below the sufficiency threshold of 50 nanomoles per litre. Prevalence rose to 63 percent among participants living above 45 degrees latitude and sampled in winter.

Magnesium insufficiency affected 28 percent of participants. Iron deficiency affected 19 percent of women of reproductive age against 3 percent of men. Vitamin B12 deficiency affected 11 percent overall, concentrated in participants over seventy and those following plant-based diets without supplementation.

## Fat-Soluble Vitamins

Vitamins A, D, E, and K require dietary fat for absorption, and this dependency is stronger than most people assume.

The Brightwater authors ran a sub-study on vitamin D3 supplementation and found that taking the supplement with a meal containing at least 11 grams of fat improved absorption by 32 percent compared with taking it on an empty stomach. The practical implication is that timing a vitamin D supplement to the largest meal of the day is a free improvement over taking it first thing in the morning with water.

Because they are stored in adipose tissue rather than excreted, fat-soluble vitamins accumulate. This makes toxicity possible in a way it generally is not for water-soluble vitamins. Vitamin A is the main concern, with chronic intakes above 3,000 micrograms of retinol per day associated with liver damage and reduced bone density.

## Absorption Inhibitors and Enhancers

Micronutrient absorption depends heavily on what else is in the meal.

Non-heme iron, the form found in plants, is poorly absorbed on its own at roughly 2 to 5 percent. Vitamin C consumed in the same meal can increase this three to four-fold by reducing ferric iron to the more absorbable ferrous form. This is the mechanism behind the traditional pairing of lentils with tomatoes or spinach with lemon.

Working against this, phytates in whole grains and legumes bind iron and zinc, and polyphenols in tea and coffee inhibit iron absorption by up to 60 percent when consumed with a meal. The Brightwater authors noted that simply moving tea and coffee to between meals rather than with meals was the single highest-impact change available to iron-deficient participants.

Calcium and iron compete for the same absorption pathway, which is why calcium supplements and iron supplements should be separated by at least two hours.

## Supplements Versus Food

The Brightwater Survey found that isolated supplements reliably corrected measured blood levels but did not consistently reproduce the health outcomes associated with obtaining the same nutrients from food.

Beta-carotene is the clearest illustration. Diets high in beta-carotene-rich vegetables are consistently associated with lower cancer risk, yet isolated beta-carotene supplementation has shown neutral to harmful results in trials involving smokers.

The authors' position was that supplements are appropriate for correcting a documented deficiency, and poorly supported as a general substitute for dietary patterns.
